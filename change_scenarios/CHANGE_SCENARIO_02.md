## 1. Trigger and new information

Ruth reviewed the collected web copy, Service macro, branch pad and warehouse instruction together. She will not sign a combined message that implies every refund can wait for warehouse inspection or that “refunded” is always equivalent to money received by the customer.

The trigger is a cross-functional wording review, not a change in legislation. The existing pack already contains different case types and clocks; Ruth is now making her approval position explicit. She wants current official-text checks and specific factual questions before an authoritative policy is issued.

## 2. Written review comments

1. Differentiate online cancellation, fault/remedy enquiries, incorrect/damaged goods and voluntary goodwill. A commercial 28-day headline is not a universal eligibility rule.
2. Establish which factual dates and evidence matter for the applicable case. In particular, review how dispatch evidence may affect the analysis instead of assuming only depot receipt can matter.
3. Do not use a Northbank working-day target to redefine an applicable statutory calendar-day limit.
4. Do not describe an accepted or attempted provider instruction as confirmed customer receipt without an appropriate basis.
5. Identify who can approve policy interpretation, customer wording, financial release and a genuine exception. One sponsor's funding approval does not settle all four.

These are review concerns and constraints. They are not a completed legal decision table, a set of future user stories or a definitive assessment of the supplied cases.
