## How to interpret this pack

The coordinator has retained uncertain statements and disagreements. An interviewee's requested feature, explanation or proposed target is not an approved requirement. Formal responsibility is described where known; participants must create their own stakeholder assessment, influence analysis, elicitation plan and validation record. No power/interest ratings, future RACI or completed requirements are supplied.

The interviewer prompts are part of the received source record. They do not replace the participant's own follow-ups. A facilitator should answer further questions only from supplied sources, or record the answer as unavailable. Do not invent stakeholder consent or sign-off.

## Organisational relationships supplied by the sponsor

Helen Ward, Operations Director, reports to the Managing Director. Aisha Khan leads Customer Service and Tom Bell leads warehouse operations within Helen's remit. Jo Evans manages branch S07 and reports through Store Operations. Priya Shah, Finance Manager, reports to the Finance Director and controls central refund release. Ben Cole, Ecommerce Manager, reports to the Commercial Director. Oliver King, IT Manager, reports to the Finance Director for technology spend. Ruth Lane is the retained Compliance Adviser; she can require legal review of customer-facing terms but does not authorise capital expenditure.

These reporting relationships do not establish who should own a future end-to-end service. Helen is accountable for the commissioned investigation. The board approves funding. Functional managers retain their control responsibilities. Customer participants provide experience rather than internal approval. Store colleagues outside S07, payment-provider support and information governance have not yet been interviewed.

## 1. Aisha Khan — Customer Service Manager

**Responsibilities and immediate pressures.** Aisha schedules 12 colleagues across calls, webchat and email, manages escalations and prepares the contact-demand report. She is judged on queue availability, complaints and quality monitoring. She can authorise modest goodwill within the service procedure, but cannot alter legal eligibility or central Finance controls. A reduction in contact volume could improve capacity; it might also threaten the backfill request she has been trying to secure. She wants that distinction recognised.

**Interviewer:** What happens when a customer asks where their refund is?

**Aisha:** “An adviser opens ServiceDesk and looks at the return, then the order. If the last note says sent to Finance, they often choose the refunded label because that looks closest. The customer's question is whether the money is back. The system question is whether someone completed our part. We use the same word for both. I do not want advisers making promises on a screen they do not understand.”

**Interviewer:** Which part of the experience is hardest?

**Aisha:** “It is explaining why we cannot see something the customer can prove. In R0851 the customer had a receipt from S07 on 2 June. Our central view was still waiting for goods. The customer emailed on 5 June, used webchat on 7 June and emailed again on 13 June. On the third contact we could see a provider instruction, but we still could not show how the store receipt became a warehouse record. Three colleagues reconstructed the same story.”

**Interviewer:** Does that prove the store hand-off causes the delays?

**Aisha:** “It proves we could not explain that one case. Tom says the parcel references were incomplete. Jo says they sent what the form asked for. I have not seen enough branch cases to say all stores behave that way. We do see postal returns too, and customers ask about those. Please do not build a solution for one channel and call it solved.”

Aisha estimates a “where is my refund?” call can take twelve minutes, but admits she usually remembers escalated calls rather than routine contacts. The dataset's handling minutes include several channels and must be checked before using her estimate as a universal cost. Formal complaint flags are not applied consistently: an adviser may resolve a dissatisfied contact without classifying it as a complaint.

**Interviewer:** What would you ask a system to do?

**Aisha:** “Tell us what we know, what we are waiting for and who should act. I would like automatic updates, but not an automatic promise that money has arrived. A customer must still be able to talk to someone. Webchat should not say go to the store if the store has no authority. And I do not want another dashboard we have to keep up to date by hand.”

**Interviewer:** Who should change customer promises?

**Aisha:** “Ecommerce owns the website. We own the email macros. Stores print notices. I can change a macro quickly, but Ben says that creates inconsistency with the website. Ruth wants to review wording, which is sensible, but we need a route for urgent corrections. At present the fastest route is whoever answers my message.”

**Unresolved at interview.** Aisha cannot provide a reliable unique-customer complaint count, confirm whether all outbound work is logged, or identify a formally agreed customer-visible clock. She offers two advisers for one workshop, not full-time project support. She wants any pilot to measure total adviser effort and transfers, including the work created by exceptions. Her request for a single view does not settle which system should host it or who must maintain its underlying events.

## 2. Tom Bell — Warehouse Manager

**Responsibilities and pressures.** Tom manages the leased distribution centre. Goods receipt, inspection, stock disposition and outbound fulfilment compete for shared labour. Returns are commercially important, but outbound delivery misses are reviewed every morning. Tom is concerned that a returns project will promise inspection targets without funding cover.

**Interviewer:** Does every return need a warehouse inspection?

**Tom:** “Our desk instruction says postal returns need an inspection before the refund file. That is what my staff have been taught. Online goods brought into a store arrive in the same area, and we inspect those too. I cannot tell you whether a competent store inspection could count instead. Priya says she would accept evidence if it met the policy. That is not the same as saying my team can stop tomorrow.”

**Interviewer:** Explain the dates for R0851 and R0855.

**Tom:** “R0851 was accepted in the branch on 2 June and has an inspection entry on 9 June. The export calls the first date receipt. My workbook uses receipt to mean our depot receipt. Those are different. R0855 is a sealed personal-care item with a store receipt on 3 June and an inspection entry on 11 June. We do not have a separate reliable column for every stage between the branch counter, cage collection and my bench. Do not subtract those dates and label the whole interval warehouse labour.”

**Interviewer:** Are missing references widespread?

**Tom:** “Some store cages contain labels with an order number only. We can often find the return by searching the order, but the customer may have returned one item from a basket. A colleague checks the photograph or phones the store. The queue does not show that as rework; it just looks like an item waiting. We know R0860 needed a manual match. That does not mean every manual match was wrong or every automatic link was correct.”

Tom says a barcode would help, then adds that stores cannot always print a label when the lookup screen is unavailable. He initially asks for mandatory barcode scanning on all returns; when challenged about a customer arriving without a pre-registered case, he says there must be a controlled way to create or recover the record. He has not specified who would own that exception or which evidence would be enough.

**Interviewer:** What is the staffing constraint?

**Tom:** “Four colleagues rotate through this work; they are not all inspecting continuously. In August I lose two agency shifts a week. I will not claim a new screen saves one person's salary when that person still has to unpack, inspect and put stock somewhere. We should measure touch time, elapsed waiting and identification work separately. I have estimates but no complete time study.”

**Interviewer:** How should failed payments appear to you?

**Tom:** “They should not send the same item back for inspection just because Finance's payment failed. Our physical job may be complete even though the customer still needs action. If a case reopens, tell us why. At the moment a reopened task can look like a new parcel.”

**Unresolved at interview.** Tom cannot demonstrate a complete arrival log for consolidated branch transfers, or confirm that every inspection entry records the person and evidence. He asks for a representative peak-day observation and agrees to expose missing information rather than fill it retrospectively. He disputes Aisha's shorthand “warehouse delay” but agrees customers experience the full wait regardless of internal attribution.

## 3. Priya Shah — Finance Manager

**Responsibilities and pressures.** Priya controls central refunds, reconciliation and exception escalation. She is accountable to the Finance Director for duplicate payments, audit evidence and spend. She has already challenged Helen's replacement-system assumption. Her team is small and the project must not simply move missing-information chasing into Finance.

**Interviewer:** What does the board's refund measure show?

**Priya:** “The workbook groups requests by month but takes accepted instructions observed by 30 June. The mean from authorisation to first accepted provider instruction is 2.5 days for April, 2.2 for May and 1.9 for June. It is calendar days, not working days. I did not call that customer receipt. Once people put 'refund speed' on the slide, the interpretation escaped the definition.”

**Interviewer:** What is sufficient authority to release payment?

**Priya:** “We need an identifiable order, the appropriate evidence and a decision from someone with authority. I do not personally require a second warehouse inspection for every store-accepted item. I do require an auditable basis for treating a store decision as sufficient. Jo's handwritten initial is sometimes all we get. Ruth needs to settle which evidence is appropriate for which rights and which goodwill discretion.”

**Interviewer:** Why did you circulate the £100 email?

**Priya:** “There were two suspicious store goodwill claims in May. I asked for £100 pending agreement of revised controls. The signed handbook says £150. I can see that an email from Finance can be read as a new policy even though the operations document was not amended. I expected managers to escalate the uncertainty, not create their own threshold. I cannot prove every branch received it.”

For R0855, the requested goods amount is £139. Priya says the branch queried whether local approval was enough; her note does not establish that approval authority was the only source of elapsed time. She asks the BA to distinguish proposed financial controls, published policy and observed behaviour.

**Interviewer:** Explain R0852 and R0857.

**Priya:** “R0852 had a rejected instruction on 19 June. We sent a replacement on 23 June and have a settlement confirmation dated 26 June. A Service label set at the first attempt would have been misleading. R0857 had an accepted £399 instruction and another submission blocked against a duplicate reference on the same day. A blocked attempt is not a second payment. If someone sums attempts as refunds, they will overstate the money.”

**Interviewer:** What will Finance fund?

**Priya:** “The envelope is £80,000 one-off and £15,000 annual incremental cost. £8,000 is committed already. A quote of £40,000 is not the cost of an option if integration, training, support and backfill are outside it. Nor is an internal-tools option free. I want cash and capacity treated honestly. Call minutes times £24 an hour is a capacity value, not an automatic redundancy saving.”

**Unresolved at interview.** Priya cannot identify the full cost of card disputes or lost repeat purchases attributable to returns. She has no evidence that the existing tools cannot work. She will review an options case and a control demonstration, but has not approved a vendor, future rule, benefits claim or early go-live. She says an October pilot could be acceptable with explicit scope and reconciliation evidence; she rejects an approval request based only on screenshots.

## 4. Ben Cole — Ecommerce Manager

**Responsibilities and pressures.** Ben owns online conversion, trading content and OrderHub supplier relationships. He is concerned about a confusing return experience but fears that warnings and longer promised times will harm conversion. He supported the original portal idea and is politically invested in a visible customer-facing improvement.

**Interviewer:** Where did “within five days of receipt” come from?

**Ben:** “We shortened a long help article. I thought receipt meant we had the information needed to pay; customers may read it as their parcel being accepted. The review email did not define the event. Service has another macro and stores have printed text. I am willing to change the wording, but I want a promise we can keep rather than a disclaimer no customer understands.”

**Interviewer:** Could the site show real-time return status?

**Ben:** “The supplier demo did. Oliver says it was a seeded response, not our production connection. I asked for real time because it sounded like the answer to 'where is my refund'. Perhaps the customer needs a reliable next update rather than every internal status. I need someone to help us distinguish that. Please do not turn my request into an assumed integration commitment.”

Ben wants customers to register a return through their order account and obtain a reference before dispatch. He also wants stores to accept walk-ins. When asked how a store should handle a customer who has not created an online case, he says it should be “instant and frictionless”, then acknowledges that some identity, order and item checks remain necessary. He has not defined acceptable matching risk, accessibility needs or fallback operation.

**Interviewer:** What about cancellation, faulty goods and product exclusions?

**Ben:** “The website currently leads with a 28-day returns headline because that is simple. Legal text sits below it. I do not want the BA to use that headline as the rule for every case. We sell sealed personal-care items and bundles. Our template wording may not explain those well enough. Ruth should confirm it, but she cannot attend every trading meeting.”

For R0853, Ben initially says the parcel was not back until 13 June, so “the clock cannot start earlier”. When asked about the dispatch evidence logged on 4 June, he says he does not know the legal significance and asks Ruth to review it. For R0858, he cannot determine whether the delivery charge should be included without knowing the order and the reason for the customer's entitlement. He asks the BA not to infer a full order from one returned line.

**Interviewer:** How much change can your team support?

**Ben:** “One content editor and a shared analyst can help. September has campaign work. I would rather pilot a narrow status view properly than migrate every historic record in a rush, but Helen expects a visible improvement. If we choose current tools, I need to explain why that is still a credible investment outcome.”

**Unresolved at interview.** Ben has not obtained a binding interface quote or demonstrated a complete return journey. He cannot supply an attributable conversion or repeat-purchase loss. He wants future communications to be understandable on a mobile screen, but has not validated language, timing or accessibility. His preferred portal is a hypothesis that must compete with other approaches.

## 5. Jo Evans — Store Manager, S07

**Responsibilities and pressures.** Jo leads a busy city branch with a mixed customer base and a small stockroom. Her team deals with a queue at the counter, online order lookups and physical goods that may not belong to the branch assortment. She represents one branch, not all 18.

**Interviewer:** What is different about online goods?

**Jo:** “A store sale can usually be located on the till and refunded by someone with the right access. A web purchase can be found in the lookup screen, but we cannot release the central card payment there. We take the goods, write a receipt and email Service. Customers do not see why their item is here but the website still says send it back.”

**Interviewer:** What did the customer receive for R0851?

**Jo:** “A branch receipt dated 2 June. It showed the order and item, not a reliably printed return reference. The goods went into the transfer cage. I cannot prove from the current form exactly when each parcel left the branch. Tom says it reached inspection on 9 June. The customer should not have to understand our transfer schedule to get a truthful update.”

Jo says receipt, eligibility and payment authority are often collapsed into one conversation at the counter. A colleague may accept physical custody while saying that a policy decision remains pending. The receipt wording does not make the distinction clear. She does not want a future workflow to refuse physical acceptance solely because a back-office system is offline, but also does not want staff promising refunds they cannot authorise.

**Interviewer:** Which approval threshold do colleagues use?

**Jo:** “The handbook by the till says £150. Finance emailed £100. For R0855 at £139 we asked Service because no one wanted to override Finance. My deputy used the printed handbook on another day. I cannot say which approach was formally correct. Please do not label every local decision fraud; people are trying to do the right thing with conflicting instructions.”

**Interviewer:** Would you accept mandatory digital capture?

**Jo:** “If it takes ninety seconds and helps the customer, probably. If it takes five minutes and depends on the manager's laptop, the queue will force a workaround. We have one shared lookup terminal at the service point. We need to know who can correct a wrong item and what to do without a printer. Training has to reach weekend colleagues, not just a Monday manager call.”

Jo requests “the same process for all returns”, but later clarifies that she means a consistent customer explanation and ownership. She does not necessarily mean identical eligibility, authorisation or payment steps for a faulty electrical product and an unwanted store purchase. That clarification is a source statement, not a completed design.

**Unresolved at interview.** Jo cannot quantify average handling time across all branches, confirm the courier transfer interval for every site or demonstrate that local records always carry an attributable inspection. She can offer S07 for a contained trial if Store Operations agrees cover. A two-store pilot would need a contrasting branch, not simply another cooperative manager.

## 6. Oliver King — IT Manager

**Responsibilities and pressures.** Oliver owns integration support, access administration and technical supplier review. He reports technology spend through Finance. He wants fewer fragile spreadsheets but is wary of buying another application to reconcile.

**Interviewer:** Is the current technology incapable?

**Oliver:** “That has not been established. ServiceDesk has a configurable queue and custom fields. OrderHub can provide a nightly read-only export. We may be able to surface some reference and exception information without replacing everything. I have not costed or load-tested it, and the current contract does not give us real-time write-back. 'Existing feature' does not mean 'already implemented for this business'.”

**Interviewer:** Why do statuses conflict?

**Oliver:** “Several are free text or manually selected. A return ID can be created in ServiceDesk after a store receipt exists. DepotSheet may have an order reference. FinancePay can then use its own batch reference. We can map many records, but we have not agreed which event each customer-facing word should describe. A connector will move ambiguity faster if we leave that unresolved.”

Oliver describes a nightly export at 02:00 UK time, branch till exports after close and a separate provider response file. Failed imports go to a shared technical mailbox. The mailbox alert does not ensure someone has reconciled every business exception. He does not have a documented end-to-end recovery demonstration for a missed file and replay.

**Interviewer:** What would you need from the BA?

**Oliver:** “Explain the operational need and the consequence of delay. Give us the important exceptions, data meaning, roles and evidence required. If you say 'real time', tell us which decision cannot wait and how old information may be before someone is misled. If you say 'single source of truth', tell us which facts are authoritative where. I need failure behaviour and reconciliation, not only a happy-path screen.”

**Interviewer:** Are supplier demonstrations reliable?

**Oliver:** “A demo can be useful, but it is not an interface commitment. The portal we saw used fabricated immediate order responses. The quote excludes a connector and support assumptions. I want to test duplicate messages, wrong references, delayed store receipts, provider rejection and access restrictions. We cannot infer resilience because the screen loaded once.”

He says a current-tools configuration might use two internal analyst days a week for six weeks, but no resource commitment has been approved. Changes also compete with a security patch programme in September. He can supply a more detailed technical assessment after the BA clarifies priorities.

**Unresolved at interview.** Oliver has not confirmed interface capacity, full licence coverage, record-retention controls or the scope of data migration. He asks for a named operational owner for exceptions and customer messaging. He does not support Helen's assumption that replacement is necessary, but will not promise that configuration alone is sufficient. He expects an explicit trade-off between timing, scope and assurance.

## 7. Ruth Lane — Compliance Adviser

**Responsibilities and pressures.** Ruth reviews customer terms and advises on consumer-rights and data-handling implications. She works part-time under retainer, so requests need focused questions and evidence. She cannot certify an entire future solution from a brief workshop.

**Interviewer:** Can the project simply use a single 14-day or 28-day rule?

**Ruth:** “No. We need the factual case type and applicable framework. Online cancellation, a fault report, a delivery problem and voluntary goodwill are not interchangeable. A customer may have rights even if the till receipt is missing, and a goodwill policy does not replace statutory rights. I need current official sources and a clear description of what happened before advising on the particular case.”

**Interviewer:** What concerns you in the current instructions?

**Ruth:** “The blanket statement that every refund waits for warehouse inspection is too broad to accept without review. So is the website's five-day wording when nobody agrees what receipt means. We should examine dispatch evidence and the relevant conditions, not assume the physical goods must always reach a particular team first. Do not turn this conversation into a legal decision table; I have not reviewed every case.”

Ruth refers the BA to the official consumer-contract and consumer-rights sources in the reference register. She asks for the version/date and the question being investigated. She explicitly distinguishes operational targets from legal time limits. A working-day target set by Northbank must not be substituted for a statutory calendar-day rule.

**Interviewer:** How would you look at the sample exceptions?

**Ruth:** “R0853 has a request, dispatch evidence and later receipt. I would need to know what that evidence actually proves and the relevant cancellation facts. R0856 concerns an opened sealed product; the category label alone does not settle whether an exception applies. R0858 is a partial-order record; do not refund or withhold a delivery charge by copying a whole-order rule without checking the facts.”

**Interviewer:** Do stronger controls necessarily slow the process?

**Ruth:** “A control can clarify evidence and avoid repeated chasing. It can also create a queue if nobody can approve an exception. Finance's fraud concern is legitimate, but a cash threshold is not a customer-rights decision. We need appropriate authority and evidence without using a control as a reason to ignore obligations.”

Ruth wants an audit trail of who decided, on what evidence and under which policy version. She does not demand unlimited retention or unrestricted access to customer material. Screenshots, emails and payment references should be handled for an approved purpose and available only to relevant roles. A supplier contract permitting unrelated reuse would require review.

**Unresolved at interview.** Ruth has not signed revised policy, confirmed that any supplied case breached law, approved future data retention or endorsed supplier terms. She asks the BA to collect specific applicability questions, distinguish missing facts from system gaps and allow time for policy review before wording changes. Her involvement is essential but does not make her the owner of operational capacity or investment.

## 8. Customer discussion — two separate synthetic perspectives

**Participants:** “Customer A”, an online buyer represented by R0851, and “Customer B”, represented by R0852. These are labelled composite accounts created for the simulation; neither speaks for all customers. The coordinator has removed names and contact details. The participants did not review Northbank's systems.

**Interviewer:** What did you expect when you returned the item?

**Customer A:** “I bought online because it was convenient. I took the item to the store because I was nearby. The colleague took it, gave me a slip and said the team would deal with it. I read that as the business having it back. When a later message said awaiting goods, I thought it had been lost. I do not care which warehouse owns the next step, but I do need to know whether I must do anything.”

**Interviewer:** Would another status page have helped?

**Customer A:** “Only if it agreed with the receipt in my hand. If it said the branch accepted the goods and someone was reviewing the refund, that would be more useful than a tick saying received somewhere else. I would not want to register again just to ask a question. A friend cannot easily use online forms; a phone route matters.”

**Interviewer:** What did you understand by a refund being sent?

**Customer B:** “I expected the payment was on its way. Later I was told there had been a problem. I cannot tell whether that means my bank, your payment system or a person forgot to do something. Once I explained the history, the next adviser asked me to explain it again. A clear explanation and a date for the next update would have helped, even if you could not promise the exact bank day.”

The linked source record for R0852 contains a rejected instruction on 19 June and an accepted replacement on 23 June. Its settlement confirmation is dated 26 June. Customer B's recollection does not establish what every earlier adviser could see, or whether an automated notification was sent. Those questions remain open.

**Interviewer:** What would make a faster process feel unfair?

**Customer A:** “Being told it is too late by one channel after another channel said I could bring it in. Or being asked for something impossible after you have accepted the goods.”

**Customer B:** “A system refusing to let a person explain an unusual problem. I do not expect staff to pay anyone who asks, but I would want someone to tell me what evidence they need and why.”

The participants are not asked to specify internal controls or a future platform. They want certainty about custody, understandable messages, an achievable next action and a way to challenge a problem. They also mention the inconvenience of holding onto packaging and transport to a store. No price sensitivity, willingness-to-pay or representative satisfaction score was collected.

**Unresolved at discussion.** The accounts cannot establish prevalence or quantify churn. They do not validate a prototype or give permission for future marketing use. The BA should decide what additional research would be needed, how to include different access needs and how to separate what customers value from a particular feature requested by management.

## 9. Cross-functional workshop extract — 13 July

**Helen:** “I need one process and one status. Can we agree that?”

**Priya:** “I can agree that the customer should not be misled. I cannot agree one eligibility rule or one approval for every situation.”

**Jo:** “If my team accepts the goods, the customer should not be told we have not received them.”

**Tom:** “Then record store custody separately from depot inspection. Do not use my inspection timestamp as the only receipt event.”

**Ben:** “Would customers understand those distinctions? We cannot expose ten internal codes.”

**Aisha:** “We need an explanation and the next action, not ten codes. Someone must own the update when nothing changes.”

**Oliver:** “I can move fields. I cannot determine their meaning or who corrects them.”

**Ruth:** “And wording cannot redefine a customer's rights.”

The chair did not record an agreed future-state process or approved requirement. The group asked the BA for a structured playback distinguishing objectives, factual events, customer communications, decision authority and outstanding questions. Priya requested costs for at least a credible current-tools approach and a purchased capability; Helen asked that the October pilot remain visible in the trade-offs. The minutes contain no approval to buy a system or remove an existing control.
