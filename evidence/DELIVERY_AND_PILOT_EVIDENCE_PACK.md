# Delivery experiment, operator evidence and readiness challenge

**Mettelo Business Analyst Project Studio · MTL-BA-041 · Stage 6**  
**Prepared and released:** 24 August 2026 · **Author:** programme coordinator, with supplier and operational notes  
**Important:** isolated synthetic experiment, not an approved design or operational go-live.

## 1. Why the participant receives this material

Helen asked the supplier to demonstrate the limited task/status view it had previously proposed. The coordinator arranged a small experiment while the BA's options work continued. It uses FlowReturn's prototype, regardless of which approach the participant recommends.

The demonstration was not commissioned as the participant's solution. Do not reverse-engineer a requirements baseline to make the prototype pass, or assume it is the “right” answer. A participant can reject it, recommend further evidence, use some findings to challenge another approach or conclude that a readiness decision cannot yet be made.

Your own requirement acceptance criteria and UAT approach should be recorded **before** reviewing observed results. The supplied material contains no completed UAT plan, expected-result catalogue, pass/fail assessment, severity decisions or release recommendation.

## 2. Experiment setup and scope

Two experienced Service advisers and two S07 colleagues volunteered. They were shown 48 historical synthetic cases, R0851–R0898, first using a baseline replay interface on 17 August and then a prototype view on 19 August. There are **48 paired tasks and 96 observation records**, not 96 independent users or 96 new customer returns.

The operator_group field identifies a work context (service/store), not an individual person. The same historical case facts appear in both modes through paired_case_id. Session order was not randomised; staff knew some cases from the earlier session. A supplier representative assisted selected tasks. No real payment was instructed and no actual customer was contacted.

The baseline replay approximates existing screen navigation; it is not measured live business-as-usual operation. The prototype uses imported case data and synthetic responses. It does not connect to production OrderHub, TillPoint or FinancePay. Only one desktop browser and the S07 network profile were exercised. No peak load, access-token expiry, mobile, screen-reader or disaster-recovery exercise was conducted.

## 3. What the supplier configured

The prototype imports a nightly order/return file into a searchable queue. An operator can find a return, see selected goods/reference information and update a task note. Supplier default text includes awaiting_goods, review_needed and refund_sent. Those labels have not been approved by the policy or operational owners.

A local mapper translates fields from the frozen source CSV. The mapper's logic has not been signed off and is not supplied as an approved future lifecycle. Some displayed information reflects the time of import rather than the latest business event. No authoritative customer-entitlement decision is generated by this demonstration.

The supplier has not delivered the unavailable real-time write-back or rejection-event capabilities described in Change Notice 03. A screen demonstration must not be treated as evidence that those interfaces exist.

## 5. Observer notebook — selected scenes

### Scene D01 — store receipt with a later central view

A store colleague presents the equivalent of R0851's branch acknowledgement. The prototype view still uses awaiting_goods after an older import. The supplier says a more recent file would change it. The colleague asks what to tell a customer now, rather than tomorrow. No agreed staleness threshold or customer wording is supplied.

### Scene D02 — provider rejection and replacement

The coordinator replays the R0852 history including the rejected 19 June attempt and later accepted replacement. An operator sees refund_sent before opening a separate response detail. Aisha asks whether the primary view could distinguish what is known about an instruction from settlement. The supplier says custom text and an adapter would be additional work. The observer does not classify the issue or decide its severity.

### Scene D03 — duplicate reference

The R0857 replay includes one accepted and one blocked attempt. An operator initially interprets the two rows as two refunds. Priya asks how a future user would see the relationship without authorising another attempt. No duplicate loss occurred in the isolated trial; no broad fraud-control assurance can be inferred.

### Scene D04 — ambiguous policy decision

The R0856 opened-seal case is placed in a general review queue. The supplier's demonstrator asks whether to mark it declined to complete the task. Ruth says that a product category is not a substitute for the appropriate applicability review. The observer stops short of recording an eligibility outcome.

### Scene D05 — access and audit

A store-context account can open a central case note outside the demo branch filter. A configuration change is made during the session, but the observer cannot retrieve a complete before/after record showing author, timestamp and old value. The supplier says an audit feature may be available in another licence tier. Its cost and suitability are not confirmed.

### Scene D06 — missed file and recovery

The coordinator withholds a synthetic export, then provides the next one. The queue loads, but there is no supplied reconciliation showing which cases/events were missing or replayed. The supplier describes the import as successful because the file was processed. Oliver asks what evidence would prove business completeness. No recovery test result is supplied.

## 6. Supplier note and stakeholder response

The supplier reports that operators preferred the search screen and suggests it supports the earlier claim of up to 30% fewer refund-status contacts. The experiment did not observe live contact demand, actual settlement or customer behaviour. No contractual benefit guarantee exists.

The original setup/subscription figures remain the raw commercial starting point. The £18,000 connector allowance and the new £4,500 one-off/£1,500 annual response adapter estimate have not become included merely because a demonstration took place. Training, assurance, data work and other exclusions still require costing.

Aisha says the view may reduce searching but warns that inaccurate confident messages can create repeat work. Jo values a visible reference but wants a workable offline counter route. Priya asks for business reconciliation and evidence of appropriate authority. Oliver asks for a representative failure/replay demonstration. Ruth has not approved the policy wording. Helen asks whether a narrower experiment or another option would support a credible October pilot.

None of those comments constitutes sign-off.

## 7. Follow-up material for solution evaluation

Service provided two **additional weekly aggregate observations**, separate from the 1,400-case frozen population:

| Week and observation group | New requests | Accepted instructions during week | Open requests at end | Logged inbound refund contacts | Adviser hours, including project support |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10–16 August, proposed pilot group baseline | 40 | 35 | 18 | 29 | 22 |
| 17–23 August, same work group during experiment | 44 | 36 | 26 | 24 | 29 |

The first week began with 13 open requests. There were no withdrawals/declines in these weekly aggregates: 13 + 40 − 35 = 18; 18 + 44 − 36 = 26. Accepted instructions can relate to older cases. These flows and stocks are not interchangeable.

The second week coincided with extra adviser support and a change in product mix. The prototype was isolated; staff still used current operational tools for real work. The weekly contact change therefore cannot be attributed to a live prototype deployment. There was no random allocation, customer satisfaction sample, full cost study or measured customer-receipt outcome.

The board asks whether the experiment supports scaling, changing direction, conducting further evidence gathering or deferring a production decision. The participant must decide what can reasonably be inferred and what further evaluation would be necessary.
