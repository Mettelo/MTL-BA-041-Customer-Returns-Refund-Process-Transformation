It specifies what you must do, where each output belongs, when to prepare it and how it is reviewed. This guide's broader activity descriptions do not replace those explicit requirements.

## 1. Establish the engagement

Create your separate delivery repository first. Record your interpretation of the commission, initial questions, assumptions, success measures and discovery approach. Save a dated initiation snapshot before Stage 2. Do not copy the sponsor's preferred system into an agreed requirement.

The simulation distinguishes evidence from participant analysis. Cite source path, section and record ID where relevant. Give your own requirements, decisions, risks, changes and tests stable IDs. Keep the scheme practical and consistent; no pre-filled catalogue is supplied.

## 2. Use the stages as decision gates

**Stage 1 — initiation.** Use the business context and sponsor brief to identify what decision is being commissioned, where scope is uncertain and which stakeholders/evidence are needed. Gate: concise sponsor playback of your understanding, including assumptions and unanswered questions.

**Stage 2 — discovery and elicitation.** Read the consolidated stakeholder pack as conflicting source evidence. Create your own stakeholder analysis and elicitation record. Separate facts, wishes, interpretations, proposed controls and unknowns. Gate: a discovery playback that identifies what needs validation, by whom and why it matters.

**Stage 3 — current-state investigation.** Use the operational CSVs, evidence fragments, existing forms, policy and system sources. Reconstruct your own current-state model with exceptions. Reproduce material source figures, define analytical measures and investigate plausible explanations without treating correlation or recollection as proof. Gate: current-state playback with evidence strengths and limitations.

**Stage 4 — requirements and future state.** Develop your own future needs, rule proposals, process/data concepts, acceptance criteria, options and priorities. Include transition and non-functional considerations. Test options against the business need, cost completeness, constraints and operational adoption. Gate: save a versioned requirements/options baseline before any change notice is opened.

**Stage 5 — change/constraint events.** Release notices 01, 02 and 03 sequentially. After each, assess the effect on the saved baseline, decisions, priorities, controls, costs, dependencies, tests and benefits assumptions. A conclusion that something is unaffected still needs reasoning. Gate: a change-group decision request and a new recorded baseline; do not imply approval merely because you produced an impact document.

**Stage 6 — delivery/UAT.** Define acceptance evidence and your own scenarios before inspecting the supplied experiment observations. Then distinguish what the notebook/dataset demonstrates from what is missing. Decide how issues should be investigated, classified and taken to the relevant owner. Consider readiness, open cases, support, training, reconciliation and rollback. Gate: an assurance/readiness recommendation, which may be conditional or negative.

**Stage 7 — recommendation/playback.** Integrate the business, option, change and assurance reasoning. Explain the recommended intervention/sequence, owner decisions, residual uncertainty and evaluation plan. Gate: final playback and indexed tagged submission. A sponsor's preferred outcome is not a scoring key.

## 3. Follow-up questions and simulated collaboration

A useful request names the fact or decision needing clarification, the source conflict, the role best placed to answer and the consequence if no answer is available. Participants should choose those questions themselves.

A facilitator can respond from the supplied sources, role-play the documented perspective or confirm “not established”. If genuinely new synthetic evidence is created, label it with date, author, cohort and affected assumption, and distribute it consistently. Do not fabricate undocumented legal approval, supplier commitment or test success to close a learner's gap.

Individual participants should retain an honest question/validation log. They should not write fictional interview responses and present them as Mettelo-provided evidence. Team participants should rotate facilitation/review so the contribution record demonstrates actual individual work.

## 4. Keep models and requirements useful

Models must explain the organisation's work, information, decisions and exceptions to their intended audience. They should be consistent with source records and explicit about uncertainty. No specific notation earns marks by itself. A polished map that silently removes difficult branches is weak evidence.

Requirements need meaningful source/need, scope and testability. Identify when a stakeholder statement is unvalidated, contradictory, infeasible or a proposed design choice. Explain prioritisation rather than making every requested feature essential. Retain traceability when requirements change, split, are deferred or are rejected.

The supplied policy pack is not the future business-rule catalogue. The systems inventory is not the future architecture. The supplier notebook is not a completed UAT report. Transforming those sources into validated analysis is the participant's work.

## 5. Analyse evidence responsibly

Keep original extracts unchanged. Document joins, cohorts, cutoffs, units, handling of blanks and failed/repeated events. Separate demand, completed work and queue stock. Distinguish goods refund requested, accepted instructions, settlement evidence, compensation, capacity value and incremental cash.

Do not use a completed-case average as a universal customer outcome without qualification. Do not label a manual match an error without examining it. Do not infer all audit findings apply to the full population. Show reproducible calculations that answer a decision, rather than producing a dashboard for its own sake.

## 6. Stage distribution for Mettelo facilitators

This public repository is the **full source library**. Public folders and branches do not prevent reading ahead. For controlled cohorts, distribute only manifest-assigned files at the current stage through a private learner space. Stage 1 includes the participant instructions and provenance; Stage 2 the stakeholder pack; Stage 3 the operational sources; Stage 5 the notices in order; Stage 6 the experiment pack and observations. Stages 4 and 7 are synthesis gates without supplied BA answers.

Keep a cohort release record outside the master: pack version, file set, date issued and decision snapshot received. Do not send later notices until the participant has recorded the prior response. Names in the public manifest can reveal that changes exist; confidentiality of the narrative requires controlled distribution and the public master cannot enforce it.

## 7. Tools and final quality

A text editor, spreadsheet application and diagramming tool are sufficient; paid platforms are not required. Choose accessible formats, legible diagrams and navigable indexes. Include validation/reproduction notes so another reviewer can follow the evidence without private software access.

Use [assessment criteria](ASSESSMENT_CRITERIA.md) for a self-review of reasoning, not a document-count checklist. Follow the established Mettelo repository workflow, preserve individual contribution evidence and submit your repository URL/tag. Present the work accurately as a simulation.
