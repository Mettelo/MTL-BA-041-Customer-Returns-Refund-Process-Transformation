# Existing policy and business-rule sources

**Mettelo Business Analyst Project Studio · MTL-BA-041 · Stage 3**  
**Collated:** 20 July 2026 · **Status:** source documents with differing authority; not a harmonised policy  
The following documents are fictional Northbank sources. They do not create legislation. The participant must establish applicability, resolve authority questions and propose their own validated rules.

## 1. Document authority and unresolved ownership

Operations maintains the published returns handbook. Finance controls payment release and fraud escalation. Ecommerce owns customer-facing web text. Customer Service owns adviser macros. Compliance advises on legal applicability and reviews terms. There is no signed cross-functional document defining an end-to-end refund clock.

The coordinator found three versions of counter guidance in circulation. The sources below preserve those differences. A newer date does not automatically prove a communication was an authorised amendment. The source library does not decide which rule should govern a future solution.

## 2. Returns handbook v2.1 — signed by Operations, 1 February 2026

### 2.1 Voluntary store goodwill

For unwanted store purchases, Northbank offers a voluntary 28-day return arrangement for unused goods with satisfactory purchase evidence, subject to product-specific exclusions described to customers. The branch duty manager may authorise a goodwill refund up to £150 under the signed handbook; higher values require central Finance review.

Staff must distinguish this commercial arrangement from a customer's statutory remedies. A refusal under the goodwill arrangement must not be used as a blanket response to a reported fault. Sale pricing does not itself remove statutory rights. The policy does not require the original paper receipt as the only possible purchase evidence; unresolved evidence goes to the duty manager.

The handbook does not define how this delegation applies to an online order accepted physically in a store. It does not give branch tills the technical ability to release an online central payment.

### 2.2 Online cancellation

Advisers should identify an online cancellation separately from fault reports and discretionary goodwill. They should retain the request date, order/delivery facts, goods involved and relevant return evidence, and refer uncertain applicability to the policy owner. The supplied handbook points staff to official consumer-contract sources but does not reproduce a complete legal rule set.

The warehouse desk instruction attached to the handbook says: “For postal goods, release the refund file after inspection is recorded.” The handbook has no approved exception text for cases where different evidence or timing considerations apply. Compliance has asked to review the instruction. The pack does not approve withholding every refund until depot inspection.

### 2.3 Faults, incorrect goods and damaged delivery

Staff should capture what the customer reports and the remedy sought, retain purchase/evidence references and escalate uncertain cases. A generic 28-day goodwill refusal is not the prescribed response to every fault report. Repair, replacement, rejection and refund can involve different conditions. The organisation has not provided a complete procedure for all product categories; do not invent one and label it an existing policy.

The source CSV's reported_reason is the intake label, not a legal finding that a product was faulty or a definitive classification of entitlement. Category and opened-seal fields are not enough to decide every exception without further facts.

### 2.4 Physical receipt

A branch accepting custody should give the customer an acknowledgement with date, branch, order/item information and the colleague's identification. Acknowledging custody is not automatically authorising a refund. Postal receipt should be recorded with available parcel and order/return references. Ambiguous identity must be investigated; do not attach a parcel to an unrelated case merely to clear the queue.

The handbook does not settle whether a branch's recorded inspection is sufficient for a central online refund or how to reconcile a receipt created before a central return ID. No maximum branch-to-depot transfer interval appears in the signed version.

## 4. Finance release procedure v1.8 — 8 April 2026

Central refund files should contain the order reference, amount, authorising person, reason/evidence reference and unique payment reference. A preparer must not be the sole approver of their own manual exception. Changes to amount or payment route after approval need review by Finance. A provider response must be reconciled before an exception is treated as resolved.

An accepted provider instruction and a confirmed settlement are different events. A rejected instruction needs an accountable exception and customer communication decision. A blocked duplicate attempt must be checked before resubmission; it must not be counted automatically as another paid refund.

The source procedure does not define a universally valid “paid” customer status, a service level for reviewing every rejection, or retention periods for every supporting document. A response file appearing in a mailbox is not written evidence that a colleague reviewed each line.

## 5. Existing customer communications and service targets

The web help page published 12 May uses “refund within five days of receipt”. The Service macro says “once the warehouse confirms your return, allow up to five working days for our Finance team to process it”. A printed branch acknowledgement says “refund normally within 5 days”. No single approved definition is attached.

Helen's proposed 90% within two working days from authorisation is an operational ambition, not a signed customer promise and not a statutory deadline. It cannot be substituted for a calendar-day legal requirement where one applies. The participant must distinguish a business target, an internal queue threshold, a customer communication and a legal obligation.

The worked source forms and macros are retained in [existing forms and communications](../evidence/forms/EXISTING_FORMS_AND_COMMUNICATIONS.md). They are deliberately incomplete existing documents, not templates for the future design.

## 6. Information handling and access — existing extract

Customer contact data should be available only to roles needing it for the stated service purpose. Store staff should not see unrelated customer cases; a supplier demonstration should use synthetic records. Card details, bank account details and identity documents must not be placed in a general shared spreadsheet or this training repository.

Keep decision and reconciliation evidence according to the approved retention schedule. That schedule was requested but not provided in this simulation, so no retention duration is established by this pack. Vendor terms allowing unrelated reuse, unrestricted export or blanket retention require the appropriate review. Do not infer that unlimited retention is a control requirement.

## 7. Questions held open by the source owners

Operations has asked who should authorise a cross-channel refund when receipt and inspection evidence come from different teams. Finance has asked which evidence permits prompt release without an avoidable duplicate or wrong-amount risk. Compliance has asked how cancellation, faults and goodwill will be distinguished. Ecommerce and Service have asked who may approve urgent corrections to a misleading message.

These are unresolved questions from the source owners. They are not a completed participant gap analysis, business-rule catalogue or future decision table. Any proposed rule must be traced to its source and reviewed at the appropriate authority.
